# Grievance Redressal and Claim Disputes

## Stage 1 — Aurora customer service

Raise a grievance in the app, on the portal, by email to
grievance@aurorahealth.example, or on the 24×7 helpline. Aurora acknowledges
within **3 working days** and resolves within **15 days** of receipt.

Quote your policy number and, for a claim dispute, the claim reference number.

## Stage 2 — Grievance Redressal Officer

If you are not satisfied with the Stage 1 outcome, or receive no response within
15 days, escalate to Aurora's Grievance Redressal Officer. The GRO responds
within **14 days**. The GRO's contact details are printed on the policy schedule
and published on the portal.

## Stage 3 — Insurance Ombudsman

If Aurora's final response is unsatisfactory, or Aurora has not responded within
30 days of the original complaint, you may approach the Insurance Ombudsman for
your region.

- The Ombudsman handles disputes where the claim value does not exceed
  **₹50,00,000**.
- You must approach the Ombudsman within **1 year** of Aurora's final rejection.
- The Ombudsman must pass an award within **3 months** of receiving all
  submissions.
- The award is binding on Aurora. It is not binding on you; you retain the right
  to go to a consumer forum or civil court.
- There is no fee, and you do not need a lawyer.

You cannot approach the Ombudsman on a matter already before a court, consumer
forum, or arbitrator.

## Common grounds of claim rejection and what to do

| Reason given | What usually helps |
|---|---|
| Non-disclosure of a pre-existing condition | Medical records showing first diagnosis date after policy inception |
| Treatment within a waiting period | Evidence of continuous prior coverage that carries the waiting period |
| Not medically necessary / could have been out-patient | A written justification from the treating doctor for admission |
| Hospital does not meet the definition | Registration certificate and bed count from the hospital |
| Documents incomplete | Supply the specific document; the 45-day query clock applies |

A rejection letter must state the specific policy clause relied on. If it does
not, ask for it in writing before escalating — a rejection without a cited
clause is the single most common thing the Ombudsman overturns.
